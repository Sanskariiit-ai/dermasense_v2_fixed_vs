Eczema (atopic dermatitis)

Eczema is a common long-term skin condition that causes dry, itchy, inflamed skin. It often starts in childhood but can appear at any age. It tends to come and go, with flare-ups followed by calmer periods.

Typical features: dry skin, intense itching (often worse at night), red or darker patches, and skin that can crack, ooze or thicken from scratching. Common areas include the inner elbows, behind the knees, hands, neck and face.

People with eczema often have a personal or family history of allergies, asthma or hay fever. Flare-ups can be triggered by irritants such as harsh soaps, hot water, wool, fragrances, sweat, stress and dry weather.

General care: moisturise often (thick creams or ointments work better than thin lotions), bathe in lukewarm rather than hot water, use gentle fragrance-free cleansers, and avoid known triggers. A doctor can advise on prescription creams and other treatments. See a doctor if the skin is weeping, crusting, very painful, looks infected, or sleep is disturbed.
