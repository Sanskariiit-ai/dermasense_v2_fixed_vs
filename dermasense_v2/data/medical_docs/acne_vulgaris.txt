Acne vulgaris

Acne happens when hair follicles become blocked with oil and dead skin cells and may become inflamed. It causes blackheads, whiteheads, red pimples, and in more severe forms painful deep lumps (nodules or cysts). It most often affects the face, chest and back and is common in teenagers but also affects adults. Hormonal changes are a common influence.

General care: wash the face gently twice a day with a mild cleanser, avoid scrubbing, use non-comedogenic (non-pore-clogging) products, and do not squeeze or pick spots because this can cause scarring and infection. Over-the-counter products containing benzoyl peroxide or salicylic acid can help mild acne.

See a doctor if acne is painful, deep, leaving scars, affecting your mood, not improving after several weeks of self-care, or appeared after starting a new medicine. Some acne medicines are not safe in pregnancy, so tell your doctor if you are pregnant or planning pregnancy.
