Psoriasis

Psoriasis is a long-term condition in which skin cells build up faster than normal. This causes raised, red or darker patches (plaques) covered with thick silvery-white scales. It is not contagious.

Common areas: elbows, knees, scalp and lower back, though it can appear anywhere, including nails. Plaques may itch, sting or crack. Some people also develop joint pain, stiffness or swelling (psoriatic arthritis), which needs medical review.

Flare-ups can be linked to stress, infections, skin injury, some medicines, smoking and heavy alcohol use. Psoriasis tends to run in families.

General care: keep skin moisturised, avoid scratching and skin injury, and manage stress. Treatments range from prescription creams and light therapy to tablets and injections, so a dermatologist assessment is recommended. Seek prompt care for painful swollen joints, widespread redness covering most of the body, or signs of infection.
